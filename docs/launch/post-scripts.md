# Sixteen ready-to-record videos

Default end card: **Mealsolved · Android testing · Link in profile**. Use this only once the signup destination is verified. Captions below are publication drafts, not posts already sent. Record actions honestly; never stage app functionality that does not exist.

## V01 — Dinner after a long shift

**Cover:** Dinner after 12 hours?

**0–3s:** Keys land on counter. Say: “After twelve hours at work, dinner shouldn't be another hard decision.”
**3–8s:** Open fridge, then show phone. “I don't need a hundred ideas. I need a few I can actually make.”
**8–21s:** Tap Hungry Now; show three cards and select one. “Mealsolved gives me three quick meal ideas. I pick one and get on with dinner.”
**21–30s:** Show a meal you actually cooked. “We're testing it on Android. Join through the link in our profile.”

**Caption:** The hard part isn't always cooking. Sometimes it's choosing. Mealsolved is in Android testing—join through our profile.

## V02 — One good option, two wrong ones

**Cover:** Keep one. Refresh two.

**0–3s:** Screen showing three choices. “One looks good. These two don't.”
**3–8s:** Hold the preferred card. “So I keep this one.”
**8–20s:** Refresh; point to the retained card and new options. “And refresh the others. My favorite stays while I look for something better.”
**20–28s:** Founder or app screen. “Would this make picking dinner easier?”

**Caption:** You don't have to lose a good idea to find another one. Keep a choice, refresh the rest. Would you use this?

## V03 — The dinner conversation

**Cover:** “What do you want?”

**0–7s:** Two people, or one person playing both roles: “‘What do you want for dinner?’ ‘I don't know. What do you want?’”
**7–18s:** Show Help Me Decide. “Let's start with three choices instead.” Show selecting one together.
**18–27s:** “Dinner decided. Send this to the person you have this conversation with.”

**Caption:** The nightly conversation that gets us nowhere. Start with three options and choose together.

## V04 — Founder invitation

**Cover:** Why I'm building Mealsolved

**0–8s:** Founder on camera. “I'm building Mealsolved to make one everyday question easier: what should I eat?”
**8–20s:** App footage over narration. “Three ideas, filters when you want them, and a planner when you've found something you like.”
**20–32s:** Founder. “It's in Android testing. I want honest feedback from people who'll use it for real meals. Join through our profile.”

**Caption:** I'm Wyatt, the person building Mealsolved. Help me make dinner decisions easier. Android testers welcome through the link in our profile.

## V05 — Sandwiches count

**Cover:** This counts as dinner.

**0–4s:** Put a sandwich and chips on a plate. “Dinner doesn't have to be a project.”
**4–16s:** Show assembling your actual sandwich, then a matching recipe in the app. “Sometimes something familiar and simple is exactly what you need.”
**16–26s:** “What's your easiest real-life dinner?”

**Caption:** A sandwich counts. So does breakfast for dinner. What's the meal you make when your energy is gone?

## V06 — Chicken, dinner, done

**Cover:** Chicken in the fridge?

**0–4s:** Show chicken safely packaged or a text hook. “Know the ingredient, but not the meal?”
**4–20s:** Help Me Decide → single filter button → Dinner and Chicken → Apply. “Choose what you're in the mood for. Get a few ideas instead of scrolling forever.”
**20–30s:** Show the resulting choices. “Try it in our Android test—link in profile.”

**Caption:** Start with what you know: chicken, dinner, maybe a time limit. Let Mealsolved help with the decision.

## V07 — Breakfast for dinner

**Cover:** Breakfast. At dinner.

**0–4s:** Show actual eggs/toast or pancakes. “Who decided breakfast had a bedtime?”
**4–18s:** Short cooking shots, then find the matching breakfast recipe. “Familiar ingredients. A meal I actually want. That works for me.”
**18–27s:** “Sweet breakfast or savory breakfast for dinner?”

**Caption:** Breakfast for dinner deserves a spot in the rotation. Are you choosing eggs and toast or pancakes?

## V08 — First-place cooking

**Cover:** First kitchen. Simple dinner.

**0–4s:** One pan and basic ingredients. “Your first kitchen doesn't need a chef's menu.”
**4–20s:** Make one verified easy recipe from the app; show the actual steps without inventing a cook-time claim. “Start with one meal you like, ingredients you recognize, and instructions you can follow.”
**20–30s:** “What was the first meal you learned to cook?”

**Caption:** Start with one meal. Repeat it. Build from there. What would you teach someone in their first apartment?

## V09 — Plan three dinners

**Cover:** Three dinners. One sitting.

**0–4s:** Founder: “Future me would appreciate having dinner ideas ready.”
**4–22s:** Add three recipes to different dates; show the real planner. “I'm choosing a few meals now so I have somewhere to start later. I can change them when plans change.”
**22–31s:** “Would you plan three days or the whole week?”

**Caption:** A little planning can be enough. Start with three dinners instead of trying to organize your entire life.

## V10 — Save the idea to a day

**Cover:** Found dinner? Give it a day.

**0–4s:** Home recipe card. “That looks good. I don't want to forget it.”
**4–20s:** Tap Plan directly on the card, choose day and meal, then verify in planner. “Add it to the day you want it. No need to open every recipe first.”
**20–27s:** “Save this for your next meal-planning session.”

**Caption:** Spot a meal, choose a day, keep moving. Quick-add to the planner is built for those little decisions.

## V11 — Cooking for more than one

**Cover:** Who's eating tonight?

**0–5s:** Two plates. “Picking for yourself is one thing. Picking for a household is another.”
**5–22s:** Use consenting demo household profiles; show selecting whom to cook for and existing preferences. “Choose who you're cooking for so their saved preferences can help shape the ideas.”
**22–31s:** “What's the hardest part of choosing a meal everyone wants?”

**Caption:** Different preferences, one dinner. Tell us what makes household meal decisions difficult. Always check ingredients yourself for allergies.

## V12 — Answer a real question

**Cover:** You asked: [actual question]

Use only an actual question with permission if identifying its author. If none exists, title it “How does refreshing work?” without implying a testimonial.

**0–5s:** “How do I keep one recipe and change the others?”
**5–21s:** Demonstrate hold and refresh. “Hold the one you want to keep, then refresh. Release it when you're ready for all-new choices.”
**21–30s:** “What else would you like me to show?”

**Caption:** A quick Mealsolved walkthrough. Leave a question and I'll show the actual steps.

## V13 — Repeat the winning hook

**Cover:** Use the Week 1–2 winning theme.

Default if results are inconclusive: remake V01 with “Hungry. Tired. No idea what to make.” Keep the same app demonstration and invitation, but use a different real meal and a tighter 20-second edit. Change the hook only when comparing performance; do not change every variable at once.

**Caption:** Hungry and out of ideas? Start with three. Join our Android test through the link in our profile.

## V14 — Sweet Treat

**Cover:** Dinner is done. Something sweet?

**4–21s:** Tap Sweet Treat and show the actual three results: hot, cold, quick/easy when available. “A few ideas to start from. Keep one you like and refresh the rest.”

**Caption:** Something sweet without another endless search. Which would you choose: hot, cold, or quick and easy?

**0–5s:** Two labeled glasses you actually made, or show the category without pretending to have cooked. “Which mood are you in?”
**5–20s:** Open Smoothies & Shakes, select a recipe, show its ingredients. “Browse the category and find something that sounds good.”
**20–28s:** “What's your go-to flavor?”

**Caption:** Chocolate shake, fruit smoothie, or something else? Tell us your go-to combination.

**Caption:** Building this around real meal decisions. This month's lesson: [insert a verified finding]. What should we pay attention to next?
